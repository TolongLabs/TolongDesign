## What it is

Pinterest is a massive visual discovery engine and digital bookmarking platform
founded in 2010 by Ben Silbermann, Evan Sharp, and Paul Sciarra. While widely
recognized as a consumer lifestyle service, it is also extensively used across
the design and technology industry as an ideation and visual indexing tool.

The platform allows users to save "pins" (images, animations, infographics, and
links) onto thematic "boards". Its recommendation algorithms analyze visual
features and user affinities to surface related imagery across web design,
graphic design, typography, brand packaging, UI kits, and interior architecture.

In client-facing web development, Pinterest excels during early discovery.
Because clients and non-technical stakeholders are almost universally familiar
with Pinterest boards, teams can collaborate on shared mood boards to align on
color palettes, illustration styles, and overall visual tone before committing
to concrete UI wireframes.

## Paywall and pricing

- **Free:** Personal and business accounts are completely free to create and
  use. Users can save unlimited pins, create unlimited boards, invite
  collaborators, and perform visual searches at no cost.
- **Paid:** No subscription fees for platform usage. Monetization is driven
  entirely by advertising (Promoted Pins and shopping campaigns for brand
  advertisers).
- **Sign-up required?** Yes. While limited public landing pages are viewable,
  active searching, pin expansion, and board curation require a free registered
  account.
- **Licence for client work:** Pins are uploaded and linked by global users and
  remain subject to original copyright holders. They are intended for internal
  moodboarding and creative reference, not direct reproduction.

## How to use

1. Set up a shared project board: Create a secret or public board for the
   TolongLabs project (e.g., "Client Brand Direction — 2026").
2. Query specific design terminology: Use precise search queries (e.g.,
   "brutalist typography poster", "minimal fintech landing page", "bento grid
   ui").
3. Use the visual lens tool: Click the magnifying icon on any pin's corner to
   trigger visual similarity searches across textures, color balances, or layout
   structures.
4. Synthesize client mood boards: Export key pins into project discovery briefs
   to establish agreed-upon aesthetic boundaries before starting component
   design.

## When to use it

- Use it in early discovery phases to gather broad aesthetic inspiration with
  non-technical clients and brand stakeholders.
- Use it to explore texture, physical print layouts, photography lighting, and
  color palettes.
- Skip it when you need production-ready frontend code, verified component
  tokens, or modern SaaS UI flows; use
  [Landingfolio](../design-galleries/landingfolio.md) or
  [Are.na](../design-galleries/are-na.md) instead.

## Agent notes

- Poor agent-friendliness: Pinterest enforces aggressive login walls, anti-bot
  fingerprinting, and client-side rendering, making automated scraping
  impractical.
- Agents should treat Pinterest as a human collaboration venue where designers
  and clients gather early visual consensus.
- Never scrape or commit raw image assets from Pinterest into project
  repositories.
