## What it is

Laws of UX is a widely respected digital reference catalog created by designer
and author Jon Yablonski. It translates fundamental principles of psychology,
cognitive science, and human-computer interaction into actionable, visual
heuristics that digital designers and engineers can apply directly to
interfaces.

The platform categorizes laws into distinct functional areas:

- **Heuristics:** Rules of thumb governing usability and ergonomics (e.g.,
  Fitts's Law, Hick's Law, Jakob's Law, Miller's Law, Postel's Law, Tesler's
  Law).
- **Principles:** Core human perception behaviors (e.g., Aesthetic-Usability
  Effect, Doherty Threshold, Pareto Principle).
- **Gestalt Principles:** Visual perception laws (e.g., Law of Proximity, Law of
  Similarity, Law of Common Region, Law of Uniform Connectedness).
- **Cognitive Biases:** Psychological tendencies affecting user decisions (e.g.,
  Peak-End Rule, Serial Position Effect, Von Restorff Effect, Zeigarnik Effect).

Each law is presented with a striking geometric illustration, a concise
one-sentence takeaway, practical key takeaways, origins and historical context,
and recommended further reading.

## How to use

1. Audit UI layouts for ergonomics: Apply Fitts's Law
   (`https://lawsofux.com/fittss-law/`) to verify that primary action buttons
   are appropriately sized and easily targetable, especially on mobile screens.
2. Defend design decisions: Use Jakob's Law ("Users spend most of their time on
   other sites") to justify standard navigation patterns instead of confusing,
   overly idiosyncratic client navigation schemes.
3. Reduce cognitive load: Apply Hick's Law and Miller's Law to simplify complex
   form fields, breaking multi-step checkout processes into manageable chunks.
4. Integrate into design reviews: Use the card summaries as a standard checklist
   during design QA and heuristic evaluation sessions.

## Examples of Key Laws

- **Fitts's Law:** The time to acquire a target is a function of the distance to
  and size of the target.
- **Jakob's Law:** Users spend most of their time on other sites, meaning they
  prefer your site to work the same way as all the other sites they already
  know.
- **Hick's Law:** The time it takes to make a decision increases with the number
  and complexity of choices.

## When to use it

- Use it in client presentations or PR descriptions to provide rigorous,
  psychologically grounded justifications for UI simplicity.
- Use it as an objective checklist during usability audits and design critiques.
- Skip it if you need copy-paste React components or CSS code; use component
  libraries or [Codrops](../reading/codrops.md) instead.
